# Redline Run Music Brief

## Purpose

Define the music identity for Redline Run so a dedicated track can be generated in Suno and later saved as `audio/redline-run.mp3`.

Tone target: practical, vivid, music-first, and game-aware. The brief should produce a song that works as music first and a gameplay instrumental second.

## 1. Track Role

Redline Run is the speed-first dare track.

It should feel clearly different from Sunset Highway:

- Darker, faster, cleaner, and more dangerous.
- Neon city/expressway instead of sunset road trip.
- Less open-road warmth, more tunnel-light pressure.
- More precision and temptation, less relaxed arcade cruise.
- Built for "one more try" speed challenges like Redline Warmup, Redline Dare, The Dare, and Fuel Panic.

The emotional role is not "fun car song." It is the moment when the game leans forward: late night, glass towers, wet asphalt, hot brakes, red lights, and a driver choosing the dangerous line because it feels good.

## 2. Music Goal

The song should support a 45-75 second high-speed arcade challenge, but it can be generated as a full 2-minute song so the best section can be edited, looped, or trimmed later.

The race uses these gameplay sections:

- Launch
- Groove
- Redline / Pressure
- Open Pull / Breather
- Final Dare / Final Push

The music should have clear enough section changes that the instrumental feels like it is helping the road shape itself. It should not require exact beat sync, rhythm-game timing, or audio analysis. The goal is a track with a strong emotional arc that the Road Director can sit on top of.

## 3. Genre Range

Do not restrict Redline Run to generic racing EDM. Good options include:

- Dark synth-rock: urgent vocal, dirty bass synth, sharp guitars, live or live-feeling drums.
- Industrial synthwave: mechanical pulse, metallic percussion, harsh arps, neon dread.
- Electro-punk: fast, lean, hooky, slightly reckless, less glossy.
- Arcade metal/synth hybrid: guitar attack plus retro-future synth leads and big drums.
- Fast funk-rock: tight bass movement, bright snare, restless guitar, danceable without becoming disco.
- Cyberpunk surf-rock: tremolo guitars, sprinting drums, noir melody, neon coastal danger.
- Aggressive 90s arcade breakbeat: chopped drums, distorted bass, siren synths, coin-op intensity.
- Dark pop-rock instrumental after vocal stem removal: strong melodic vocal shape first, clean instrumental bed later.

## 4. Recommended Main Direction

Recommended direction:

A vocal-first dark synth-rock / arcade-industrial track that starts restrained, builds quickly, opens into a fast groove, drops into a short breather, then explodes into a final dare section.

Why this direction:

- It gives Suno a song-shaped target instead of a loop-shaped target.
- Vocals help Suno create melody, contrast, and section identity.
- The instrumental can later carry gameplay after vocal removal.
- Synth-rock keeps Redline distinct from Sunset Highway without collapsing into generic EDM.
- Arcade-industrial textures match Redline Run's cleaner but more dangerous expressway identity.

Production center:

- Pulsing bass synth.
- Tight live drums or live-feeling hybrid drums.
- Distorted guitar accents, not constant wall-of-guitar mud.
- Sharp synth arps and siren-like hooks.
- Close urgent vocal in the full song version.
- Clear transient beat for racing feel.
- Short intro, fast arrival.

## 5. Structure Map

| Time | Gameplay Section | Music Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00-0:15 | Launch | Restrained ignition: pulse, low synth, tight kick, a vocal line or hook fragment, immediate momentum with no long cinematic wait. |
| 0:15-0:45 | Groove | Main speed pocket: bass locks in, drums open, guitars/synths establish the fast expressway identity. |
| 0:45-1:15 | Redline / Pressure | Heavier traffic energy: sharper drums, brighter arps, more urgency, vocal hook or chant pushes the edge. |
| 1:15-1:30 | Open Pull / Breather | Short release: thinner texture, more air, night-drive lift, still moving forward. |
| 1:30-2:00 | Final Dare / Final Push | Explosive last section: bigger drums, synth sirens, guitar bite, highest speed, no fade-out that weakens the finish. |

## 6. Suno Prompt

Use this as the primary Suno prompt. It is written for a full song with vocals first, so vocals can later be stripped and the instrumental can be tested in-game.

```text
Vocal-first dark synth-rock / arcade-industrial song for a neon city expressway at midnight, full 2-minute structure. Start tense and restrained with pulsing bass synth, dry kick, low arps, and a close urgent vocal. Build quickly into a clean high-speed groove with distorted guitar accents, tight live drums, metallic synth hits, and no generic EDM drop. Lyrics feel like chasing the red line, city lights smearing, speed as temptation, staying just ahead of consequence; no novelty race-car lyrics. Around 1:15, open into a brief weightless pullback, then explode into a final dare with bigger drums, shouted hook, synth sirens, and guitar bite. Clear section changes, strong beat, cinematic, playable, not muddy.
```

Prompt length: 715 characters including spaces and punctuation.

## 8. Lyric Direction

The lyric should support the mood without turning into a novelty racing song.

Strong themes:

- Late-night redline drive.
- Chasing the edge because stopping would feel worse.
- Neon road and city pulse.
- Speed as temptation.
- The city as pressure, not scenery.
- A driver pulled between control and surrender.
- Consequence staying just behind the headlights.

Avoid:

- Literal "race car" language.
- Checkered flags, trophies, engines roaring as the whole idea.
- Cartoon danger.
- Generic "drive fast tonight" filler.
- Brand names or car specs.
- Lyrics that only work because the listener knows the game.

Good lyric posture:

- First person or close second person.
- Short urgent phrases.
- Physical images: red light, glass, tunnel, rain, heat, white lines, pulse, teeth, signal, siren.
- Emotional subtext: temptation, pressure, risk, focus.

Possible hook territory:

- "Don't let off now."
- "I can feel the red line."
- "The city wants a piece of me."
- "One more light and I disappear."
- "Hold it straight through the siren."

These are direction markers, not mandatory lines.

## 9. Instrumental Gameplay Notes

The final instrumental needs to work under engine, boost, warning, crash, and UI sounds.

Required traits:

- Strong beat that reads immediately at race start.
- Clear section changes for Launch, Groove, Redline / Pressure, Breather, and Final Dare.
- Enough dynamic contrast that the race does not feel flat.
- Final Push energy that feels bigger than the Groove.
- Clean low end that does not blur fuel warnings or crash feedback.
- Midrange hooks that survive vocal removal.
- Not too vocal-dependent; the instrumental should still have a lead identity.
- No long intro that delays gameplay.
- No quiet breakdown long enough to make the road feel dead.
- No overly dense cymbal wash or guitar mud.
- No huge reverb tail that smears loop/edit points.
- A usable 45-75 second excerpt should be extractable if the full 2-minute song is too long.

Preferred gameplay edit:

- Keep the first playable beat within the first 2-4 seconds.
- If trimming, preserve Launch into Groove and at least one pressure lift.
- If looping, avoid a soft fade-out. Cut on a strong bar or make a purpose-built gameplay edit later.

## 10. File Plan

When ready, the final dedicated Redline audio should be saved as:

```text
audio/redline-run.mp3
```

The game already references that path for Redline Run and falls back safely to `audio/sunset-highway.mp3` if the Redline file is missing.

File preparation notes:

- Export as MP3 after selecting the final Suno generation or instrumental stem.
- Test loudness against `audio/sunset-highway.mp3`.
- Check that music does not overpower SFX, fuel warnings, boost, crash, countdown, or finish cues.
- Keep the original vocal version archived separately if the instrumental stem is used for gameplay.
- Do not replace Sunset Highway audio.
- Do not change game code just to add the track file later.
